The Bourbon Cup

Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 August 2026

The Bourbon Cup is a scoring app for one annual golf tournament played by a field of roughly sixteen people in Gaylord, Michigan. It is not a service sold to anybody. It is run by the tournament's own director, and this policy describes what it stores, why, and how to have it removed.

The short version. Signing in stores your email address and an account identifier so the app knows which name on the tournament roster is yours. Everything else it holds is golf: scores, matches, handicaps, photos you post, and money owed to the director for the trip. Nothing is sold, nothing is shared with advertisers, and there is no analytics or tracking of any kind. You can delete your account from inside the app at any time.

Who is responsible

The app is operated by the tournament's director, who can be reached at aarondjensen@gmail.com. That is also the address for any question about this policy or any request covered by it.

What is collected, and why

WhatWhere it comes fromWhy
Email address and account identifier Google or Apple, when you sign in To recognise you on your next visit and to link your account to your name on the tournament roster. If you sign in with Apple and choose Hide My Email, the app receives Apple's relay address and never sees your real one.
Name, team, handicap and GHIN number Entered by the tournament director, before you ever sign in They are the tournament's entry list. Handicap indexes are looked up from the USGA's GHIN service using the number the director enters.
Scores, matches, closest-to-the-pin, skins and card signatures Typed into the app by you and by the people you are playing with They are the tournament. Scoring is communal — one phone in a group usually posts for all four players.
Photos you post Chosen by you from your device The photo library for that year. Photos are resized on your device before upload and stored with the identifier of the account that posted them.
Notification token, and basic device facts Your browser, if you turn notifications on To send push notifications. Stored alongside it: the browser's user-agent string, whether the device is iOS, and whether the app is installed to the home screen — kept only because the usual support question is "why didn't I get that notification" and those three facts are the answer.
Trip payments Entered by the tournament director A record of what each player owes for the house and the greens fees, and what they have paid. Amounts, dates and a note — no card numbers, no bank details. The app processes no payments.

What is not collected

  • No analytics, no advertising identifiers, no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No location. The app never asks for it and never records where a phone is.
  • No contacts, calendar, microphone or camera roll access beyond the single photo you pick when you post one.
  • No payment or financial account information.

Who can see it

Everyone in the tournament can see the golf. Scores, the draw, handicaps, photos and the results of every year are shared with the whole field, which is the point of the app. The leaderboard is readable by anybody with a link, and the app has a guest mode that lets somebody look around without an account at all — so treat anything you post as visible.

Your email address is not. It is shown only to the tournament director, on the admin screen where they can see which account is attached to which name.

Your balance is shown only to you and to the director. No screen shows one player another player's money.

Where it is stored, and who else touches it

Data is stored in Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore, Firebase Authentication, Cloud Storage and Firebase Cloud Messaging), on Google's infrastructure in the United States. Google acts as the app's hosting provider. The app is served by Vercel. Handicap lookups are requests to the USGA's GHIN service using a GHIN number the director has entered.

No data is sold, rented, or shared with anybody else — no advertisers, no data brokers, no analytics companies. It would be disclosed only if the law required it.

How long it is kept

The tournament's record is kept indefinitely, because it is a record: the app holds every cup back to 2016 and the point of it is that those years can still be read. Your account is kept only as long as you keep it — see below.

Deleting your account

In the app: More → My Account → Delete Account. It is immediate and it needs no email to anybody. Full instructions, and the same request by email if you can no longer sign in, are on the account deletion page.

Deleting your account removes your sign-in, your email address, the link between your account and your name on the roster, and every notification token registered to you. Your name stays on the tournament roster, with the scores under it and the cards other players signed — those are a record of rounds that other people played in and attested to, and removing them would rewrite a result the field agreed on. The roster row is unlinked from you and free for somebody to claim.

If you want the name itself removed as well, ask the director at the address above.

Your choices

  • Notifications can be turned on and off per type in My Account, and off entirely in your device's settings.
  • Photos you posted can be deleted by you from the Photos tab.
  • A copy of what the app holds about you, or a correction to any of it, can be requested at the address above.

Children

The app is for the adults playing in one golf tournament. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will be mentioned in the app.
